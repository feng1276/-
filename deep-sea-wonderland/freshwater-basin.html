Freshwater Basin

From mountain headwaters to sprawling wetlands — explore the rivers, lakes, and marshes that sustain terrestrial life and connect continents to the sea.
从山间源头到广袤湿地——探索维系陆地生命并将大陆连接至海洋的河流、湖泊和沼泽。

Amazon River Ecosystem / 亚马逊河生态系统

The world's largest river by discharge volume — home to one in ten known species on Earth.
世界上流量最大的河流——地球上十分之一的已知物种以此为家。

🐟 Red-Bellied Piranha / 红腹食人鱼

Contrary to their fearsome reputation, piranhas are primarily scavengers and foragers. They use their razor-sharp interlocking teeth to nip fins, strip carcasses, and crack seeds. They school for protection — not aggression — as they themselves are prey to caimans and dolphins.
与其可怕的名声相反，食人鱼主要是食腐动物和觅食者。它们用锋利的互锁牙齿咬切鱼鳍、剥净尸体和咬开种子。它们集群是为了保护——而非攻击——因为它们自己也是凯门鳄和海豚的猎物。

⚡ Electric Eel / 电鳗

Not a true eel but a knifefish, the electric eel can deliver shocks of up to 860 volts — enough to stun a horse. Specialized electric organs, made of modified muscle cells called electrocytes, comprise 80% of its body. It uses low-voltage pulses for navigation in murky water.
电鳗并非真正的鳗鱼而是刀鱼，可发出高达860伏特的电击——足以击晕一匹马。由称为电细胞的改性肌肉细胞组成的专门电器官占其身体的80%。它使用低电压脉冲在浑浊水中导航。

🐟 Arapaima / 巨骨舌鱼

One of the world's largest freshwater fish, reaching 3 metres and 200 kg. Arapaima are obligate air-breathers — they surface every 5-15 minutes to gulp air into a modified swim bladder that functions like a lung. Their armour-like scales resist piranha bites.
世界上最大的淡水鱼之一，可达3米长、200公斤重。巨骨舌鱼是专性空气呼吸者——它们每5-15分钟浮出水面吞入空气，进入功能如肺的改良鱼鳔。它们铠甲般的鳞片能抵御食人鱼的撕咬。

Lake Ecosystem Layers / 湖泊生态系统分层

From sunlit shallows to the dark depths — understanding the vertical zoning of lake life.
从阳光普照的浅滩到黑暗深处——了解湖泊生命的垂直分区。

During summer, many temperate lakes stratify into distinct temperature layers. The thermocline acts as a barrier — the warm epilimnion above, cold hypolimnion below — and only mixes again during spring and autumn turnover, redistributing oxygen and nutrients.
夏季，许多温带湖泊分层为不同的温度层。温跃层充当屏障——上层是温暖的变温层，下层是寒冷的均温层——只有在春季和秋季翻转时才再次混合，重新分配氧气和营养物。

Wetland Importance / 湿地的重要性

Nature's kidneys — wetlands filter water, control floods, and shelter biodiversity.
大自然的肾脏——湿地过滤水体、控制洪水并庇护生物多样性。

💧 Natural Filtration / 自然过滤

Wetland plants and soils trap sediments, absorb excess nutrients, and break down pollutants. A single acre of wetland can filter millions of litres of water annually, improving water quality downstream for drinking, irrigation, and aquatic habitats.
湿地植物和土壤捕获沉积物、吸收过量营养物并分解污染物。一英亩湿地每年可过滤数百万升水，改善下游的饮用水、灌溉和水生栖息地的水质。

🌊 Flood Control / 防洪

Wetlands act like giant sponges — absorbing, storing, and slowly releasing floodwater. One acre of wetland can hold about 5.7 million litres of water. Coastal wetlands reduce storm surge energy by up to 75%, protecting inland communities.
湿地像巨大的海绵——吸收、储存并缓慢释放洪水。一英亩湿地可容纳约570万升水。沿海湿地可将风暴潮能量减少高达75%，保护内陆社区。

🐸 Biodiversity Hotspot / 生物多样性热点

Though covering only ~6% of Earth's land surface, wetlands support 40% of all plant and animal species. Migratory birds depend on wetlands as stopover sites, and countless amphibians, fish, and invertebrates breed exclusively in these habitats.
虽仅覆盖地球陆地表面约6%，湿地却支持着40%的动植物物种。候鸟依赖湿地作为中途停留地，无数两栖类、鱼类和无脊椎动物专门在这些栖息地繁殖。

🫧 Carbon Storage / 碳储存

Peatlands (a type of wetland) store twice as much carbon as all the world's forests combined — despite covering just 3% of land. When drained or burned, they release massive amounts of CO₂, making wetland conservation critical for climate stability.
泥炭地（一种湿地）储存的碳量是全世界森林总和的两倍——尽管仅覆盖3%的陆地。当被排干或焚烧时，它们释放大量CO₂，使湿地保护对气候稳定至关重要。

💰 Economic Value / 经济价值

Wetlands provide an estimated $47 trillion in ecosystem services annually — including fisheries, water purification, tourism, and storm protection. Investing in wetland restoration returns $7-10 for every dollar spent.
湿地每年提供约47万亿美元的生态系统服务——包括渔业、水净化、旅游和风暴防护。投资湿地恢复，每花费1美元可带来7-10美元的回报。

⚠️ Under Threat / 面临威胁

Approximately 64% of the world's wetlands have been lost since 1900, and the remaining are vanishing three times faster than forests. Agriculture, urban development, and pollution are the primary drivers of this catastrophic decline.
自1900年以来，全球约64%的湿地已消失，剩余的湿地正以三倍于森林的速度消失。农业、城市开发和污染是这一灾难性衰退的主要驱动因素。

Freshwater Fish Gallery / 淡水鱼画廊

From mountain streams to lowland rivers — the diverse fish that populate inland waters.
从山间溪流到低地河流——栖息在内陆水域中的多样鱼类。

🌈 Rainbow Trout / 虹鳟

A prized game fish native to North America's cold-water streams and lakes. The iridescent pink stripe along its flank gives it the name. They require clean, oxygen-rich water, making them important bioindicators of stream health.
原产于北美冷水溪流和湖泊的珍贵钓鱼对象。侧腹部闪亮的粉色条纹赋予了它名字。它们需要清洁富氧的水，使其成为溪流健康的重要生物指标。

🎣 Largemouth Bass / 大嘴鲈鱼

An apex predator in many freshwater systems, the largemouth bass can consume prey up to half its own size. Its bucket-like mouth creates a powerful suction when it strikes. Native to eastern North America, it has been widely introduced worldwide.
在许多淡水系统中是顶级掠食者，大嘴鲈鱼可以吞下自身一半大小的猎物。它桶状的嘴在攻击时产生强大的吸力。原产于北美东部，已被广泛引入全球各地。

🐱 Channel Catfish / 斑点叉尾鮰

Named for their cat-like whiskers (barbels) that are covered in taste buds — essentially swimming tongues. Catfish can "taste" their surroundings, detecting food in complete darkness. They thrive in rivers, lakes, and reservoirs throughout North America.
因其覆盖味蕾的猫状胡须（触须）而得名——基本上是会游泳的舌头。鲶鱼能"品尝"周围环境，在完全黑暗中检测食物。它们在北美各地的河流、湖泊和水库中广泛分布。

🐟 Sturgeon / 鲟鱼

Living fossils whose lineage dates back ~250 million years. Sturgeon have no scales — instead, rows of bony plates called scutes protect their body. The beluga sturgeon can reach 7 metres and produce the world's most expensive caviar. Many species are critically endangered.
活化石，其血统可追溯到约2.5亿年前。鲟鱼没有鳞片——而是由称为骨板的一排排骨质板保护身体。欧洲鳇可达7米，产出世界上最昂贵的鱼子酱。许多物种极度濒危。

Amphibians — Life at the Water's Edge / 两栖动物——水边的生活

The bridge between aquatic and terrestrial worlds — frogs, salamanders, and their kin.
水生与陆地世界之间的桥梁——青蛙、蝾螈及其亲属。

🐸 Tree Frogs / 树蛙

With adhesive toe pads and vibrant colours, tree frogs are expert climbers. Their permeable skin makes them highly sensitive to environmental changes, earning them the title "canaries in the coal mine" for ecosystem health. Over 800 species exist worldwide.
凭借黏性趾垫和鲜艳的颜色，树蛙是攀爬高手。它们渗透性的皮肤使其对环境变化高度敏感，为它们赢得了生态系统健康"煤矿中的金丝雀"的称号。全球有超过800个物种。

🦎 Spotted Salamander / 斑点钝口螈

These secretive amphibians spend most of their life underground in burrows. On the first warm rainy night of spring, they migrate en masse to vernal pools to breed — a spectacular event called "Big Night." Algae actually lives inside their eggs in a rare symbiotic relationship.
这些隐秘的两栖动物大部分时间生活在地下的洞穴中。在春天第一个温暖雨夜，它们大批迁徙到春池繁殖——一场被称为"大夜"的壮观事件。藻类实际上生活在它们的卵内，这是一种罕见的共生关系。

🌸 Axolotl / 美西钝口螈

The axolotl is a neotenic salamander — it retains its juvenile aquatic form (including external gills) throughout its entire life. Endemic to the lake complex of Xochimilco near Mexico City, they possess extraordinary regenerative abilities, capable of regrowing limbs, spinal cord, and even parts of their brain.
美西钝口螈是一种幼态蝾螈——它终生保持幼年水生形态（包括外鳃）。它们是墨西哥城附近霍奇米尔科湖群的特有物种，拥有非凡的再生能力，能够再生四肢、脊髓甚至部分大脑。

Dragonfly & Damselfly Life Cycle / 蜻蜓与豆娘的生命周期

From aquatic nymph to aerial acrobat — one of nature's most dramatic transformations.
从水生水虿到空中杂技演员——大自然最具戏剧性的转变之一。

🦟 Aquatic Ambush Predator / 水生伏击掠食者

Dragonfly nymphs (naiads) are the terror of the underwater world. They use a specialized hinged lower jaw (labium) that shoots out at lightning speed to snatch prey — including tadpoles, small fish, and mosquito larvae. A single nymph can consume dozens of mosquito larvae per day.
蜻蜓水虿（水仙子）是水下世界的恐怖。它们使用特殊铰接下颚（下唇），以闪电速度弹出捕获猎物——包括蝌蚪、小鱼和蚊子幼虫。一只水虿每天可消耗数十只蚊子幼虫。

🪲 The Final Transformation / 最后的蜕变

After 1-5 years as a nymph (depending on species), the dragonfly climbs out of the water onto a plant stem. Its exoskeleton splits open along the back, and the winged adult emerges, pumping fluid into its wings before taking its maiden flight — all within a few hours.
在水虿阶段生活1-5年后（视物种而定），蜻蜓爬出水面到植物茎上。它的外骨骼沿背部裂开，有翅膀的成虫破壳而出，在开始首次飞行前将液体泵入翅膀——所有这些在数小时内完成。

✈️ Aerial Mastery / 空中掌握

Adult dragonflies are among the most agile fliers in the animal kingdom. They can fly forward, backward, hover, and make hairpin turns. With nearly 360-degree vision from 30,000 lens facets per eye, they intercept prey in mid-air with a 95% success rate — the highest of any predator.
成年蜻蜓是动物界中最敏捷的飞行者之一。它们能向前飞、向后飞、悬停并进行急转弯。每只眼睛有30,000个透镜面，提供近360度视野，它们以95%的成功率在空中拦截猎物——是所有掠食者中最高的。

Beaver — The Ecosystem Engineer / 海狸——生态系统工程师

No other animal (besides humans) so dramatically reshapes its landscape.
没有其他动物（除了人类）能如此戏剧性地重塑其景观。

🦫 Nature's Hydrologist / 大自然的水文学家

Beavers build dams using trees, mud, and stones — creating ponds that can cover hectares. These impoundments raise the water table, recharge groundwater, reduce downstream flooding, and create complex wetland mosaics that benefit countless other species.
海狸用树木、泥土和石头建造水坝——创造出可覆盖数公顷的池塘。这些蓄水工程提高了地下水位、补充了地下水、减少了下游洪水，并创造了使无数其他物种受益的复杂湿地荟萃。

🏗️ Engineering Marvels / 工程奇迹

The largest known beaver dam — in Alberta, Canada — spans 850 metres and is visible from space. Beavers also construct lodges with underwater entrances, keeping them safe from predators. Inside, they maintain a living chamber above the waterline.
已知最大的海狸水坝——位于加拿大阿尔伯塔——横跨850米，从太空中可见。海狸还建造水下入口的巢穴，确保它们免受掠食者威胁。内部，它们在水面以上维持着一个生活室。

🔄 Keystone Species / 关键物种

Beaver wetlands provide habitat for amphibians, waterfowl, fish, and invertebrates. Studies show streams with beaver dams have 37% more biodiversity. In areas prone to wildfire, beaver ponds create natural firebreaks and refugia for wildlife.
海狸湿地为两栖动物、水禽、鱼类和无脊椎动物提供栖息地。研究表明，有海狸水坝的溪流生物多样性高37%。在易发生野火的区域，海狸池塘创造了天然防火带和野生动物的避难所。

⚠️ Freshwater Under Threat / 淡水面临威胁

Freshwater ecosystems are among the most imperilled on Earth. Freshwater species populations have declined by 83% since 1970 — faster than any other biome. Only 37% of the world's longest rivers remain free-flowing, and 1 in 3 freshwater species face extinction.
淡水生态系统是地球上最濒危的生态系统之一。自1970年以来，淡水物种种群下降了83%——比任何其他生物群落都要快。世界上最长河流中仅37%仍然畅通无阻，1/3的淡水物种面临灭绝。

💚 What You Can Do / 你可以做什么

Conserve water at home, reduce pesticide and fertilizer use, support wetland restoration projects, and advocate for the protection of free-flowing rivers. Every drop counts.
在家中节约用水，减少农药和化肥使用，支持湿地恢复项目，并倡导保护畅通河流。每一滴水都很重要。

River Ecosystems — Arteries of the Earth / 河流生态系统——地球的动脉

Rivers are not just water flowing downhill — they are complex living systems that shape landscapes and sustain civilizations.
河流不仅仅是向下流的水——它们是塑造景观和维持文明的复杂生命系统。

🌊 The River Continuum / 河流连续性

The River Continuum Concept describes how a river changes from headwater to mouth. Small, shaded headwater streams rely on leaf litter for energy. Mid-sized rivers open to sunlight, supporting algae and aquatic plants. Large, turbid lowland rivers are powered by organic matter from upstream. This gradient creates distinct ecological zones along a single river's length.
河流连续性概念描述了一条河流从源头到入海口的变化。小型阴蔽的源头溪流靠落叶残渣提供能量。中型河流向阳光敞开，支持藻类和水生植物。大型浑浊的低地河流以上游有机物为动力。这个梯度在单条河流上创造了不同的生态区域。

🔄 Nutrient Cycling / 营养循环

Rivers are critical conveyors of nutrients from land to sea. They transport nitrogen, phosphorus, and carbon in dissolved and particulate forms, fueling estuarine and coastal productivity. The Amazon alone delivers 20% of all river water entering the world's oceans, creating a productivity plume visible from space stretching hundreds of kilometres into the Atlantic.
河流是营养物从陆地输送到海洋的关键传导者。它们以溶解和颗粒形式输送氮、磷和碳，为河口和沿海生产力提供燃料。仅亚马逊河就贡献了进入全球海洋所有河水的20%，形成一个从太空中可见、绵延数百公里进入大西洋的生产力羽流。

🐟 Migratory Highways / 迁徙通道

Rivers are migratory corridors for fish like salmon, shad, sturgeon, and eels. Atlantic salmon return to the exact stream of their birth after years at sea, navigating by scent. American eels do the reverse — born in the Sargasso Sea, they migrate up rivers and live in freshwater for decades before returning to the ocean to spawn once and die.
河流是鲑鱼、鲥鱼、鲟鱼和鳗鱼等鱼类的迁徙通道。大西洋鲑出海多年后，凭借气味返回它们诞生的精确溪流。美洲鳗则相反——出生在萨加索海，它们上游迁徙并在淡水中生活数十年，然后返回海洋产卵一次后死去。

🏙️ Rivers and Civilization / 河流与文明

The world's great ancient civilizations — Egypt (Nile), Mesopotamia (Tigris-Euphrates), Indus Valley, China (Yellow and Yangtze) — all arose along major rivers. Rivers provided water for irrigation, transportation routes, fertile floodplain soils, and protein from fisheries. Today, over 2 billion people depend directly on rivers for their drinking water.
世界伟大的古代文明——埃及（尼罗河）、美索不达米亚（底格里斯-幼发拉底河）、印度河谷、中国（黄河和长江）——都沿主要河流兴起。河流提供了灌溉用水、运输路线、肥沃的洪泛平原土壤和渔业蛋白质。今天，超过20亿人直接依赖河流获取饮用水。

The Salmon's Journey — An Epic Life Cycle / 鲑鱼的旅程——史诗般的生命周期

One of nature's most extraordinary migrations — from freshwater birth to ocean wandering and an impossible return home.
大自然最非凡的迁徙之一——从淡水诞生到海洋漫游，再到不可思议的归家之旅。

🥚 Stage 1: Redd / 阶段1：鱼巢

Female salmon dig gravel nests called redds in cold, clean stream beds, depositing up to 5,000 eggs. The male fertilizes them externally. The eggs incubate for 2-5 months, depending on water temperature, and are extremely vulnerable to sedimentation — fine silt can smother them. Only about 15% survive to the fry stage.
雌性鲑鱼在寒冷干净的溪流沙砾中挖掘称为redds的鱼巢，产下多达5000颗卵。雄性体外受精。卵孵化2-5个月，视水温而定，且极易受沉积影响——细泥沙可能使它们窒息。仅约15%能活到仔鱼阶段。

🐟 Stage 2: Fry to Smolt / 阶段2：仔鱼到降海幼鱼

After hatching, alevins stay in the gravel absorbing their yolk sac. Once they emerge as fry, they feed on insects and grow rapidly. After 1-3 years in the river, they undergo smoltification — a remarkable physiological transformation that allows them to survive in saltwater. Their bodies change osmoregulation, colour, and behaviour.
孵化后，带卵黄囊的仔鱼留在沙砾中吸收卵黄。一旦出苗成为仔鱼，它们以昆虫为食并快速生长。在河中生活1-3年后，它们经历银化——一种非凡的生理转变，使它们能在盐水中生存。它们的身体改变渗透调节、颜色和行为。

🌊 Stage 3: Ocean Life / 阶段3：海洋生活

Smolts enter the ocean and may travel thousands of kilometres — Atlantic salmon journey to feeding grounds off Greenland. They grow rapidly on a diet of fish, squid, and crustaceans, gaining up to 95% of their body mass in the ocean. After 1-5 years at sea, an internal trigger (still not fully understood) signals it is time to return home.
降海幼鱼进入海洋，可能旅行数千公里——大西洋鲑旅行到格陵兰附近的觅食地。它们以鱼类、鱿鱼和甲壳动物为食快速生长，在海洋中获得高达95%的体重。在海上1-5年后，一个内部触发器（仍未完全了解）发出是时候回家的信号。

🏞️ Stage 4: The Return / 阶段4：回归

Using Earth's magnetic field, celestial cues, and an extraordinary olfactory memory imprinted at birth, salmon navigate back to their natal stream. They stop feeding, undergo dramatic physical changes (hooked jaws, humped backs, vivid colours), and fight upstream against currents, waterfalls, and predators. After spawning, most Pacific salmon die, their bodies nourishing the forest ecosystem.
利用地球磁场、天体线索和出生时铭刻的非凡嗅觉记忆，鲑鱼导航回到它们的出生溪流。它们停止进食，经历戏剧性的身体变化（钩状下颚、驼背、鲜艳颜色），逆流而上对抗水流、瀑布和掠食者。产卵后，大多数太平洋鲑死去，它们的身体滋养着森林生态系统。

Freshwater Mussels — Silent Filters, Clever Tricksters / 淡水贻贝——无声的过滤器，聪明的骗子

North America's most endangered group of animals also happens to be among its most ingenious.
北美最濒危的动物群体同时也是最聪明的之一。

🦪 Living Water Filters / 活水过滤器

A single freshwater mussel can filter up to 57 litres of water per day, removing bacteria, algae, and pollutants. Historically, dense mussel beds could filter entire river volumes in days. Their filtering clarifies water, allowing sunlight to reach submerged plants and improving habitat for fish and invertebrates.
一只淡水贻贝每天可过滤多达57升水，去除细菌、藻类和污染物。历史上，密集的贻贝床能在数天内过滤整个河流的水量。它们的过滤澄清了水体，使阳光能到达沉水植物，改善了鱼类和无脊椎动物的栖息地。

🎣 Luring Hosts / 诱骗宿主

Freshwater mussels have evolved extraordinary methods to get their larvae (glochidia) onto host fish. Some species grow a fleshy lure that mimics a small fish, complete with eyespots and a wiggling tail. When a real fish strikes the lure, the mussel releases a cloud of larvae that clamp onto the fish's gills, hitching a ride before dropping off as juveniles.
淡水贻贝进化出了非凡的方法将它们的幼虫（钩介幼虫）附到宿主鱼上。一些物种长出一个肉质的诱饵，模拟小鱼，配有眼斑和摆动的尾巴。当真鱼攻击诱饵时，贻贝释放一团幼虫，它们夹在鱼的鳃上，搭便车后作为幼体脱落。

📊 Biodiversity Crisis / 生物多样性危机

Of the ~300 freshwater mussel species native to North America, over 70% are at risk of extinction. Dams block host fish migrations, pollution kills sensitive juveniles, and sediment smothers beds. Mussel declines are a warning sign of sick rivers — and a call to action for watershed protection.
北美本土约300种淡水贻贝中，超过70%面临灭绝风险。水坝阻止了宿主鱼的迁徙，污染杀死了敏感的幼体，沉积物窒息了贻贝床。贻贝数量下降是河流病的警告信号——也是一次流域保护的号召。

🔬 Environmental Monitors / 环境监测者

Because mussels are stationary filter feeders that live for decades, their tissues accumulate pollutants over time. Scientists use mussels as "biosentinels" — analysing contaminant levels in their flesh to monitor water quality and identify pollution sources. A healthy mussel population is the best indicator of a healthy river.
由于贻贝是固定的滤食动物，寿命长达数十年，它们的组织会随时间累积污染物。科学家将贻贝用作"生物哨兵"——分析其肉体中的污染物水平以监测水质并识别污染源。健康的贻贝种群是健康河流的最佳指标。

Freshwater Stingrays — Rivers' Hidden Danger / 淡水鳐鱼——河流的隐藏危险

The Amazon and other tropical rivers host stingrays that adapted from the ocean millions of years ago.
亚马逊河和其他热带河流栖息着数百万年前从海洋适应而来的鳐鱼。

🦈 Ancient Invaders / 古代入侵者

Freshwater stingrays (family Potamotrygonidae) evolved from marine ancestors that became trapped in South America when the Andes rose and the Amazon basin was cut off from the Pacific about 20 million years ago. They fully adapted to freshwater — unlike most freshwater elasmobranchs, they have completely lost the ability to survive in saltwater.
淡水鳐鱼（Potamotrygonidae科）由海洋祖先演化而来，当安第斯山脉上升、亚马逊盆地约2000万年前与太平洋隔绝时，这些祖先被困在南美洲。它们完全适应了淡水——与大多数淡水板鳃类不同，它们完全失去了在盐水中生存的能力。

⚠️ Venomous Defence / 毒液防御

Their tail sting is coated in venom and mucus that causes excruciating pain, tissue necrosis, and in rare cases, death. River communities in South America traditionally treat stings with hot water, which denatures the heat-labile venom proteins. The stingray's flat body and ability to bury in sand makes them nearly invisible to wading fishermen.
它们的尾刺上涂有毒液和黏液，会造成剧烈疼痛、组织坏死，极少数情况下可致死。南美河流社区传统上用热水治疗蜇伤，热水能使热不稳定毒蛋白变性。鳐鱼扁平的身体和在沙中隐身的本领让涉水渔民几乎看不到它们。

🌀 Electrosense / 电感知

Like sharks, freshwater stingrays use electroreception (ampullae of Lorenzini) to detect the faint electrical fields generated by prey buried in river sediment. This "sixth sense" allows them to hunt effectively in the often murky waters of Amazonian rivers, where visibility can be near zero.
像鲨鱼一样，淡水鳐鱼利用电感受（罗伦氏壶腹）探测埋在河流沉积物中的猎物产生的微弱电场。这种"第六感"使它们能在能见度几乎为零的亚马逊河浑浊水体中有效捕猎。

🐾 Walking Stingrays / 会走路的鳐鱼

Some species use their pelvic fins to "walk" along the river bottom — a motion that resembles a crawling gait and may be controlled by neural circuits similar to those that control walking in land vertebrates. This behaviour offers a fascinating window into the evolutionary transition from swimming to walking.
一些物种使用腹鳍沿河底"行走"——这种运动类似于爬行步态，可能由类似于控制陆生脊椎动物行走的神经回路控制。这种行为为从游泳到行走的进化过渡提供了一个迷人的窗口。

River Dolphins — Freshwater Cetaceans / 河豚——淡水鲸类

The mysterious pink dolphins of the Amazon and their kin across the world's great rivers.
亚马逊神秘的粉色江豚及其在世界各大河流中的近亲。

🐬 Amazon River Dolphin (Boto) / 亚马逊江豚（Boto）

The largest river dolphin, reaching 2.5 metres and pink in colour (males are pinker). Botos have unfused neck vertebrae, allowing them to turn their heads 90 degrees — essential for navigating flooded forests. Amazonian folklore says botos can transform into handsome men who attend village parties, always wearing a hat to hide their blowhole.
最大的河豚，长达2.5米，粉色（雄性更粉）。Botos颈部椎骨不融合，使它们能将头部转动90度——这对在淹没的森林中导航至关重要。亚马逊民间传说称boto可以变成英俊男子参加村庄派对，总是戴着帽子以隐藏其喷气孔。

🐬 Ganges River Dolphin / 恒河豚

Nearly blind, these dolphins navigate entirely by echolocation in the murky waters of the Ganges, Brahmaputra, and Indus rivers. They swim on their side, trailing one flipper along the bottom to detect prey. India declared them the National Aquatic Animal in 2009. Only ~3,500 remain.
几乎完全的瞎子，这些江豚在恒河、布拉马普特拉河和印度河的浑浊水域中完全依赖回声定位导航。它们侧着身体游泳，用一只鳍沿河底探测猎物。印度于2009年宣布其为国宝级水生动物。仅约3500只存留。

🐬 Yangtze Finless Porpoise / 长江江豚

Following the extinction of the Baiji (Yangtze river dolphin) in 2006 — the first dolphin species driven extinct by human activity — the finless porpoise is now the Yangtze's only cetacean. With fewer than 1,000 remaining, it is critically endangered. Intensive conservation efforts, including reserves and captive breeding, offer slim hope.
继2006年白暨豚（长江河豚）灭绝之后——这是第一个因人类活动而灭绝的海豚物种——江豚现在是长江唯一的鲸类。剩余不足1000只，极度濒危。包括保护区和人工繁育在内的密集保护努力提供了一线希望。

Freshwater Turtles — Ancient River Dwellers / 淡水龟——古老的河流居民

Witnesses to the age of dinosaurs, freshwater turtles continue to inhabit rivers and wetlands worldwide.
恐龙时代的见证者，淡水龟继续栖息在世界各地的河流和湿地中。

🐢 Alligator Snapping Turtle / 鳄龟

The heaviest freshwater turtle in North America, reaching 100 kg. It uses a worm-like appendage on its tongue to lure fish directly into its powerful, hooked beak. They can stay submerged for up to 50 minutes and have been recorded living over 100 years in the wild.
北美最重的淡水龟，重达100公斤。它用舌头上蠕虫状的附属物引诱鱼直接进入其强有力的钩状喙。它们可保持潜水长达50分钟，记录的野外寿命超过100年。

🐢 Mata Mata / 枯叶龟

A master of camouflage from the Amazon and Orinoco basins, the mata mata resembles a pile of decaying leaves. Its bizarre appearance — a triangular, flattened head, fringed skin flaps, and a snorkel-like nose — allows it to ambush fish by opening its huge mouth and creating a vacuum that sucks prey in whole.
来自亚马逊和奥里诺科盆地的伪装大师，枯叶龟犹如一堆腐烂的树叶。它怪异的外表——三角形扁平头、流苏状皮瓣和通气管般的鼻子——使它能够张开大嘴产生真空将猎物整只吸入，伏击鱼类。

🐢 Softshell Turtles / 鳖（软壳龟）

Lacking the hard, bony shell of other turtles, softshells have a flexible, leathery carapace that allows them to bury rapidly into sandy river bottoms. They can absorb oxygen directly through their skin and the lining of their throat, allowing extended dives. The critically endangered Yangtze giant softshell is down to just a few known individuals.
缺少其他龟类坚硬的骨质壳，软壳龟拥有柔韧的皮革质背甲，使它们能快速埋入沙质河底。它们能通过皮肤和喉壁直接吸收氧气，允许延长潜水。极度濒危的长江斑鳖只剩几个已知个体。

Dams & Rivers — The Double-Edged Sword / 水坝与河流——双刃剑

Hydropower provides clean energy, but dams fragment rivers and devastate freshwater life. Finding balance is one of this century's greatest challenges.
水电提供清洁能源，但水坝割裂河流并摧毁淡水生命。找到平衡是本世纪最大的挑战之一。

⚡ The Renewable Dilemma / 可再生能源的困境

Hydropower generates ~16% of the world's electricity and is the largest source of renewable energy. It provides reliable baseload power with low greenhouse gas emissions. However, there are now over 58,000 large dams worldwide, and another 3,700 are planned — mostly in developing nations with high freshwater biodiversity.
水电发电量约占全球电力的16%，是可再生能源的最大来源。它以低温室气体排放提供可靠基载电力。然而，全球现有超过58,000座大型水坝，另有3,700座在规划中——大多在淡水生物多样性高的发展中国家。

🐟 Blocked Migrations / 受阻的迁徙

Dams block fish migrations, with devastating consequences. The Mekong River — home to the world's largest inland fishery feeding 60 million people — faces existential threats from a cascade of mainstream dams. Fish ladders, designed for salmon in temperate rivers, are often ineffective for tropical species with different swimming behaviours.
水坝阻断鱼类迁徙，带来毁灭性后果。湄公河——拥有养活6000万人口的世界最大内陆渔业——面临着一连串干流水坝的生存威胁。为温带河流鲑鱼设计的鱼梯，对具有不同游泳行为的热带物种往往无效。

🏞️ Dam Removal Movement / 拆除水坝运动

A growing global movement advocates for removing obsolete dams to restore free-flowing rivers. Over 1,900 dams have been removed in the US alone, with dramatic ecological recoveries. The removal of the Elwha River dams in Washington state allowed salmon to recolonize 110 km of pristine habitat within just a few years.
日益增长的全球运动倡导拆除废弃水坝以恢复畅通河流。仅美国就已拆除超过1900座水坝，出现了显著的生态恢复。华盛顿州埃尔瓦河水坝的拆除使鲑鱼在短短几年内重新定居了110公里的原始栖息地。

🔄 Fish-Friendly Design / 友好鱼类设计

Innovative solutions include nature-like fishways (bypass channels mimicking natural streams), environmental flow releases that mimic natural flood pulses, and strategic dam placement to minimize impacts on the most biodiverse river stretches. The best option, where possible, remains avoiding dams on free-flowing rivers altogether.
创新解决方案包括仿自然鱼道（模仿自然溪流的旁路通道）、模仿自然洪水脉冲的环境流量释放，以及战略性地安置水坝以最小化对最具生物多样性河段的影响。在可能的情况下，最佳选择仍是在畅通河流上完全避免修建水坝。

Riparian Zones — The River's Green Ribbon / 河岸带——河流的绿色缎带

The thin strip of vegetation along a riverbank does more than look beautiful — it is essential to the health of the entire aquatic ecosystem.
河岸沿线狭窄的植被带不仅美丽——它对整个水生生态系统的健康至关重要。

🌳 Nature's Buffer Strip / 大自然的缓冲区

Riparian vegetation acts as a buffer between land and water. Tree roots stabilize banks against erosion, filter sediment and agricultural runoff before it enters the river, and absorb excess nitrogen and phosphorus. A riparian buffer as narrow as 10 metres can remove up to 90% of suspended sediment from surface runoff.
河岸植被充当陆地和水体之间的缓冲。树根固定河岸防止侵蚀，在沉积物和农业径流进入河流之前过滤它们，并吸收过量的氮和磷。窄至10米的河岸缓冲带就能从地表径流中去除高达90%的悬浮沉积物。

🌡️ Thermal Regulation / 温度调节

Shade from riparian trees keeps water temperatures cool and stable — critical for cold-water species like trout and salmon. Removing streamside vegetation can raise water temperatures by 5-10°C, stressing or killing sensitive aquatic life. Climate change makes riparian shading even more vital as a thermal refuge.
河岸树木的荫蔽保持水温凉爽稳定——对鳟鱼和鲑鱼等冷水物种至关重要。移除溪流旁植被可使水温升高5-10°C，压垮或杀死敏感水生生物。气候变化使河岸遮荫作为热避难所更加重要。

🍂 The Leaf Litter Engine / 落叶引擎

Fallen leaves from riparian trees are the primary energy source for headwater stream food webs. Aquatic insects like caddisflies and stoneflies shred leaves into smaller particles, which are consumed by collector-gatherers and filter-feeders downstream. A stream without overhanging trees loses its entire energy base.
河岸树木落叶是源头溪流食物网的主要能量来源。石蛾和石蝇等水生昆虫将叶子撕成小碎片，供下游的收集-聚集者和滤食者消费。没有遮荫树木的溪流会失去其整个能量基础。

🦅 Wildlife Corridor / 野生动物走廊

Riparian corridors serve as vital wildlife highways through fragmented landscapes. Birds, mammals, amphibians, and insects follow these green ribbons for migration, dispersal, and finding mates. In arid regions, riparian zones support biodiversity densities up to 10 times greater than surrounding uplands.
河岸走廊是贯穿碎片化景观的重要野生动物通道。鸟类、哺乳动物、两栖动物和昆虫沿着这些绿色缎带进行迁徙、扩散和寻找配偶。在干旱地区，河岸带支持的生物多样性密度比周围高地高出多达10倍。

The Tiny Architects of Freshwater — Aquatic Insects / 淡水的小小建筑师——水生昆虫

Before the dragonfly takes to the air, countless other insects build, hunt, and sculpt the underwater world.
在蜻蜓飞上天之前，无数其他昆虫在水下世界中建造、狩猎和雕刻。

🪨 Caddisfly — Underwater Mason / 石蛾——水下石匠

Caddisfly larvae construct portable protective cases from silk and whatever materials are available — sand grains, tiny pebbles, twig fragments, even gold leaf in laboratory experiments. Each species builds a distinctive case design, and the larvae can repair damage within hours. Artist Hubert Duprat famously collaborated with caddisflies to create jewellery.
石蛾幼虫用丝和任何可用的材料——沙粒、小卵石、小枝碎片，甚至在实验室实验中使用金箔——建造便携式保护壳。每个物种构建独特的壳设计，幼虫能在数小时内修复损伤。艺术家Hubert Duprat曾与石蛾合作创作珠宝而闻名。

🪲 Water Beetle — Submerged Hunter / 水甲虫——水下猎手

Predaceous diving beetles (Dytiscidae) are the tigers of pond and stream. They carry an air bubble under their wing covers (elytra) to breathe underwater and can fly between water bodies at night. Some species are so voracious they can overwhelm prey larger than themselves, including small fish and tadpoles.
掠食性潜甲（龙虱科）是池塘和溪流中的老虎。它们在翅盖（鞘翅）下携带一个气泡以在水下呼吸，夜间可以在不同水体之间飞行。有些物种如此贪食，甚至能压倒比自身更大的猎物，包括小鱼和蝌蚪。

🪰 Mayfly — The Ephemeral Beauty / 蜉蝣——短暂的美丽

Mayfly nymphs live underwater for months or years before emerging in synchronized mass emergences. They are unique among insects in having a winged sub-adult stage (the subimago or "dun") — a transitional form that moults one final time into the sexually mature adult (imago or "spinner"). Adults have no functional mouthparts and live only hours to days.
蜉蝣水虿在水下生活数月或数年，然后同步大规模羽化。它们在昆虫中独一无二，拥有一个有翅膀的亚成体阶段（亚成虫或"dun"）——一个过渡形态，最终蜕皮成为性成熟的成虫（成虫或"spinner"）。成虫没有功能性口器，只活数小时到数天。

Springs — Where Groundwater Meets the Surface / 泉水——地下水与地表相遇之处

Springs are portals from the subterranean world, where crystal-clear, constant-temperature water creates unique micro-habitats.
泉水是来自地下世界的入口，清澈恒温的水创造了独特的微栖息地。

💎 Karst Springs / 喀斯特泉

In limestone regions, groundwater dissolves vast underground cave systems. When these aquifers emerge at the surface, they create springs with remarkably stable flow and temperature — typically the mean annual temperature of the region. Karst springs are biodiversity hotspots, hosting unique species like the olm (blind cave salamander) and cave fish that evolved in total darkness.
在石灰岩地区，地下水溶解出广阔的地下洞穴系统。当这些含水层涌出地表时，它们创造出流量和温度异常稳定的泉水——通常是该地区的年平均温度。喀斯特泉是生物多样性热点，栖息着如洞螈（盲洞蝾螈）和在完全黑暗中进化的洞穴鱼等独特物种。

♨️ Hot Springs / 温泉

Geothermally heated springs support life at temperatures that would kill most organisms. Thermophilic bacteria and archaea thrive at 70-100°C, their enzymes stable at boiling point. These extremophiles gave us Taq polymerase — the enzyme that made PCR (and modern genetic testing) possible. Hot springs are also windows into the conditions where life may have originated.
地热加热的泉水在能杀死大多数生物的温度下支持生命。嗜热细菌和古菌在70-100°C下繁殖，它们的酶在沸点下稳定。这些极端微生物给了我们Taq聚合酶——使PCR（和现代基因测试）成为可能的酶。温泉也是窥探生命可能起源条件的窗口。

🌿 Oasis Ecosystems / 绿洲生态系统

In arid regions, springs create lush oases that support life in otherwise inhospitable environments. Desert pupfish species survive in isolated spring systems, each population evolving in splendid isolation for thousands of years. Many desert spring species have ranges measured in square metres — making them among the most vulnerable organisms on Earth.
在干旱地区，泉水创造出郁郁葱葱的绿洲，在其他不宜居的环境中支持生命。沙漠鳉鱼物种在孤立的泉水系统中生存，每个种群在辉煌的隔绝中进化了数千年。许多沙漠泉生物种的分布范围仅以平方米计——使它们成为地球上最脆弱的生物之一。

⚠️ Groundwater Depletion / 地下水枯竭

When aquifers are over-pumped for agriculture and cities, springs dry up. The iconic springs of the American Southwest, the Middle East, and northern China are disappearing — and with them, species that exist nowhere else. Groundwater recharge takes centuries; current extraction rates are fundamentally unsustainable in many regions.
当含水层因农业和城市过度抽取时，泉水会干涸。美国西南部、中东和中国北方的标志性泉水正在消失——随之消失的，还有别处不存在的物种。地下水补给需要数百年；当前许多地区的抽取速度从根本上说是不可持续的。
